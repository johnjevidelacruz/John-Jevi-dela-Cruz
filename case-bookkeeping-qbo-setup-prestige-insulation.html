QuickBooks Online Build: Prestige Insulation LLC (Dropship / Invoice-driven)

This build focused on minimum-viable structure for go-live: clean accounts, consistent invoicing, and a clear workflow from WhatsApp intake to QBO—without slowing down the business.

← Back to case studies

At a glance

  • Role: QBO setup + workflow design
  • Stack: QuickBooks Online, Google Sheets, WhatsApp
  • Deliverables: COA + settings, invoice workflow, tracking sheets, approval rules

Key highlights

  • Go-live oriented QBO setup
  • Standardized product naming/SKU logic
  • Documented approvals + edge cases

Context

Prestige Insulation LLC needed QuickBooks Online set up from scratch with a workflow that matched the real intake process (WhatsApp orders) and could go live immediately.

Objective

Enable invoicing, bill tracking, and reliable reconciliation from day 1—without slowing down sales operations.

Starting point

No standardized chart of accounts (COA), inconsistent product naming, and an ad-hoc way of tracking orders and payments.

Constraints

Go-live deadline required “good structure now, refine later.” Owners needed clear approval checkpoints for pricing, tax decisions, and exceptions.

Approach (what I built first)

I prioritized the minimum viable bookkeeping operating system: define the rules, configure QBO, then create a lightweight tracking layer that mirrored how the team already worked.

  • Set QBO company settings + baseline COA aligned to dropship/invoice-driven operations.
  • Standardized the initial product catalog (naming + SKU logic) so invoicing stays consistent.
  • Designed the WhatsApp → Google Sheets → QBO flow to capture order details, approvals, and fulfillment status.
  • Defined who approves what (pricing changes, tax handling, refunds/credits, write-offs).

Deliverables

Configuration + documentation that the team can run daily without guessing.

  • QBO company setup (COA, settings, customers/vendors, products/services, bank feeds as available).
  • Invoice templates and consistent product/item structure.
  • Operating sheets: order queue, purchases/receiving, sales log, payments/deposits, agent commissions.
  • Rules doc: approvals, edge cases, and what to do when something doesn’t match.

Results (current state)

Books are structured for execution: invoices go out consistently, payments can be matched, and reconciliations have a clear path instead of being a month-end mystery.

  • Clear system of record for orders and approvals.
  • Consistent item naming reduces duplicate items and reporting noise.
  • Close-prep becomes repeatable because exceptions are handled with defined rules.

Next improvements

After real transactions accumulate, the next step is refining categories based on actual activity, confirming taxability rules by state/customer, and tightening reconciliation controls (deposits, refunds, chargebacks).

Reusable checklist

If you’re doing something similar, this is the short checklist I follow to keep the work clean and close-ready.

  • Decide your system of record: where an order is “official” (WhatsApp, Sheets, or QBO)—then enforce it.
  • Lock product naming/SKU standards early to avoid duplicate items.
  • Define approvals: who can change price, tax, refunds, and write-offs.
  • Create an exceptions path (suspense account + weekly review) so unmatched items don’t pile up.
