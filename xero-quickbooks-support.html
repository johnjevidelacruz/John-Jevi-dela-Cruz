Monthly close-prep checklist

This is the “keep it close-ready” routine I use so reconciliations don’t pile up and reports stay believable.

Weekly (light)

Prevent month-end pain by catching issues early.

  • Review uncategorised and “ask my accountant” queues.
  • Clear duplicates and match payments/deposits correctly.
  • Check for missing invoices/bills tied to real activity.

Month-end (core)

The minimum set of steps to produce close-ready reports.

  • Reconcile all bank and credit-card accounts to statements.
  • Review suspense/clearing accounts and resolve or document exceptions.
  • Confirm A/R and A/P aging makes sense; flag anomalies.
  • Generate P&L and Balance Sheet for owner/CPA review with notes.

Controls that keep books clean

Simple rules that prevent chaos.

  • Define categorisation rules and owner approvals for unusual transactions.
  • Keep a short exceptions log (what, why, next action).
  • Standard naming for vendors/customers/items to avoid duplicates.

Bookkeeping FAQ

Common questions about scope, access, and what “clean books” actually means.

Can you do reconciliations and invoicing monthly?

Yes. A standard cadence is weekly coding/matching plus month-end reconciliations and a close-prep report package.

Do you file taxes or provide tax advice?

No. Tax filing and tax strategy stay with your CPA/tax pro. I keep the books accurate and close-ready so tax work is easier.

What access do you need?

Access to QBO/Xero, bank feeds (or statements), and any ops system that affects billing (CRM, scheduling, payment processor).

Typical Xero and QuickBooks workflows I run

The exact workflows depend on your stack, but these are common patterns for service businesses.

Coding & reconciliations

Regular coding of bank feeds, reconciling transactions, and cleaning up suspense accounts so your balance sheet and P&L are trustworthy.

Invoice & payment tracking

Making sure invoices go out, payments are applied, and overdue items are flagged early instead of becoming surprises.

Connecting ops to the books

Aligning job data, timesheets, or scheduling tools with Xero or QuickBooks Online so revenue recognition and costs reflect what’s actually happening operationally.
